Theoretical Aspects of Hysteresis Losses

Magnetic memory, loop area, material type, and AC loss mechanisms

1. What the B-H loop means

H is the magnetizing field applied by the coil. B is the resulting flux density inside the core. A ferromagnetic material does not follow the same path while magnetizing and demagnetizing, so the closed B-H loop records magnetic memory.

2. Energy loss from loop area

The energy dissipated as heat per unit volume per cycle is the enclosed loop area. Numerically, this app integrates the closed curve and reports the absolute area in J/m3. Power rises with frequency: P_density = W_cycle x f.

3. Remanence, coercivity, and saturation

Remanence Br is the remaining B when H returns to zero. Coercivity Hc is the reverse field needed to drive B back to zero. Saturation Bs is the upper flux-density limit where most domains are already aligned.

4. Material selection

Soft magnetic materials such as silicon steel, ferrites, Metglas, and Permalloy have narrow loops and low Hc, which reduces heat in transformers and motors. Hard magnets such as Alnico, ceramic ferrite, and NdFeB have wide loops and high Hc, so they resist demagnetization.

5. AC loss separation

Total core loss is usually treated as hysteresis loss plus eddy-current loss plus excess loss. Eddy current loss grows strongly with frequency and conductive cores; ferrites reduce it by using high electrical resistivity.

6. Domain arrows

The domain panel visualizes many microscopic magnetic regions. With weak field they remain scattered; as H increases, more arrows rotate toward the applied field. Hysteresis occurs because those rotations and domain-wall motions lag behind the applied field.
